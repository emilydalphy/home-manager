Meatloaf

Mixed in ten minutes, and cold slices make Monday's sandwiches.

  • Serves 4
  • 15 min prep
  • 55 min cooking
  • 1 hr 10 min in all

Ingredients · 4 servings

  • 1.5 lb Ground beef
  • 3/4 cup Panko breadcrumbs
  • 1/2 cup Milk
  • 2 Eggs
  • 1 Yellow onion
  • 3 cloves Garlic
  • 1/2 cup + 2 tbsp Ketchup buy 1 bottle
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce buy 1 bottle
  • 3 tbsp Brown sugar buy 1 bag
  • 1 tsp Apple cider vinegar buy 1 bottle
  • 1 tsp Salt buy 1 box
  • 1/2 tsp Black pepper buy 1 jar

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F and line a rimmed baking sheet with parchment. You’ll shape the loaf right on the sheet, so it browns all over instead of steaming in a pan.
  2. In a big bowl, pour the 1/2 cup milk over the 3/4 cup panko and let it soak for 5 minutes. That’s what keeps the meatloaf moist.
  3. Grate the onion straight into the bowl on the big holes of a box grater, juice and all, then mince in the 3 garlic cloves. Grated onion melts into the meat, so you won’t get chunks falling out when you slice it.
  4. Whisk in the 2 eggs, 2 tbsp ketchup, 1 tbsp Worcestershire, 1 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp pepper. Add the 1.5 lb beef and mix it in with your hands. Stop as soon as there are no streaks left. Any longer and the meatloaf comes out dense.
  5. Tip it onto the sheet and pat it into a loaf about 7 inches long and 3 inches tall. Bake for 35 minutes.
  6. While it’s in the oven, stir the glaze together: 1/2 cup ketchup, 3 tbsp brown sugar and 1 tsp vinegar.
  7. Spread the glaze over the top and sides and bake another 15 to 20 minutes, until the glaze is sticky. You’re cooking it to 160°F in the middle, so if you have a thermometer, check it there. No thermometer? Cut into the center: it’s done when there’s no pink left. The glaze goes on late so the sugar doesn’t burn.
  8. Let it rest for 10 minutes before you slice it, or it’ll fall apart. Wrap up any leftover slices for sandwiches tomorrow.

Natasha's and The Wholesome Dish glaze with ketchup, brown sugar and vinegar (white and red wine); Spend With Pennies uses chili sauce and ketchup, brown sugar optional, and bakes 40 minutes before glazing. Chicken Piccata

Lemon and capers turn chicken breasts into something bright in 35 minutes.

  • Serves 4
  • 15 min prep
  • 20 min cooking
  • 35 min in all

Ingredients · 4 servings

  • 1.5 lb Boneless skinless chicken breasts
  • 1/2 cup All-purpose flour
  • 4 tbsp Unsalted butter
  • 3 tbsp Olive oil buy 1 bottle
  • 1/2 cup Dry white wine
  • 2 Lemons
  • 3 tbsp Capers buy 1 jar
  • 2 tbsp chopped Fresh parsley buy 1 bunch
  • 1 tsp Salt buy 1 box
  • 1/2 tsp Black pepper buy 1 jar

Steps

  1. Everything happens fast once the chicken’s cooked, so get the sauce ready before you start. Juice 1 or 2 lemons until you have 1/4 cup, and thinly slice whatever’s left. Rinse the 3 tbsp capers and chop 2 tbsp of parsley.
  2. Slice each chicken breast in half horizontally, through the thickest part, so you end up with 4 thin cutlets. Press them flat with your palm so they cook evenly.
  3. Season both sides with 1 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp pepper. Spread the 1/2 cup flour on a plate, press each cutlet into it and shake off the extra. A light dusting is all you need. Too much goes gummy in the sauce.
  4. Heat 1 1/2 tbsp olive oil and 1 tbsp butter in a large skillet over medium-high. When the butter foams, lay in 2 cutlets and leave them alone for 3 to 4 minutes, until they’re golden underneath and let go of the pan. Flip them and cook the other side for 2 to 3 minutes. You’re cooking them to 165°F in the thickest part, so if you have a thermometer, check it there. No thermometer? Cut into one: it’s done when it’s white all the way through. Move them to a plate.
  5. Add the other 1 1/2 tbsp oil and 1 tbsp butter and cook the last 2 cutlets the same way.
  6. Keep the heat at medium-high and pour in the 1/2 cup wine (or chicken broth). It’ll hiss. Scrape up the brown bits from the bottom, where all the flavor is, and let it bubble for about a minute, until it’s cooked down by half.
  7. Add the 1/4 cup lemon juice, the capers and the last 2 tbsp butter. Swirl the pan until the butter melts into a glossy sauce, then slide the chicken back in for 1 to 2 minutes, turning it once to coat.
  8. Spoon the sauce over the chicken and scatter the parsley and any lemon slices on top. Pasta or crusty bread soaks up the extra sauce.

Natasha's uses 1/2 cup wine, 1/4 cup lemon juice and 3 tbsp capers for 6 cutlets and returns the chicken to the sauce for 2 minutes. RecipeTin Eats adds parmesan to the flour, reduces the wine by half before the lemon, swirls the butter in over low heat so it stays glossy, and says low-sodium broth stands in for the wine.

Chana Masala

Canned chickpeas make a weeknight curry in under an hour.

  • Serves 4
  • 15 min prep
  • 40 min cooking
  • 55 min in all

Ingredients · 4 servings

  • 2 cans (15 oz) Chickpeas
  • 2 Yellow onions
  • 3 Roma tomatoes
  • 1 tbsp grated Ginger buy 1 piece (2 inch)
  • 4 cloves Garlic
  • 1 Green chili
  • 3 tbsp Vegetable oil buy 1 bottle
  • 1 tsp Cumin seeds buy 1 jar
  • 1 Bay leaves buy 1 jar
  • 1/2 tsp Ground turmeric buy 1 jar
  • 1 tsp Kashmiri chili powder buy 1 jar
  • 2 tsp Ground coriander buy 1 jar
  • 1 tsp Garam masala buy 1 jar
  • 1 tsp Kasuri methi buy 1 bag
  • 1 Lemon
  • 2 tbsp chopped Cilantro buy 1 bunch
  • 1 tsp Salt buy 1 box

Steps

  1. Finely chop the 2 onions and 3 tomatoes, grate 1 tbsp ginger, mince the 4 garlic cloves and slit the green chili down one side. Drain and rinse the 2 cans of chickpeas.
  2. Heat 3 tbsp oil in a heavy pot over medium and add the 1 tsp cumin seeds and the bay leaf. After about 20 seconds the seeds will sizzle and smell toasty. That’s your cue for the onions.
  3. Add the onions with 1/2 tsp of the salt and cook for 10 to 12 minutes, stirring now and then, until they’re deep golden. Don’t rush this part. The onions are what give the gravy its body and sweetness.
  4. Stir in the ginger, garlic and green chili for about a minute, until they stop smelling raw.
  5. Turn the heat to low and add 1/2 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp Kashmiri chili powder and 2 tsp coriander. Stir for 30 seconds so they cook in the oil. If they start to stick, add a splash of water.
  6. Add the tomatoes and cook for 6 to 8 minutes over medium, mashing them with the spoon, until they fall apart and you can see oil at the edges.
  7. Add the chickpeas, the other 1/2 tsp salt and 1 1/2 cups water. Mash a few chickpeas against the side of the pot to thicken the gravy. Cover and simmer for 15 minutes.
  8. Crush 1 tsp kasuri methi between your palms over the pot and stir in 1 tsp garam masala. Simmer 2 minutes more, then turn off the heat.
  9. Squeeze in the juice of half the lemon and taste. Add the rest if it needs more brightness. Scatter over the 2 tbsp cilantro and serve with rice or roti.

What the sources agree and differ on

All three build an onion-tomato masala: whole spices or cumin seeds in oil, onions cooked golden (the step they all say not to rush), ginger-garlic, then ground spices bloomed briefly before chopped tomatoes cook down until the oil separates. Chickpeas (canned, or soaked and pressure-cooked) simmer in it with water 15 minutes, partly mashed to thicken. Swasthi's uses Kashmiri chili, turmeric, coriander and garam masala and finishes with kasuri methi and optional amchur; Tea for Turmeric starts from 3/4 cup dried chickpeas soaked overnight and simmers 25-30 minutes; Piping Pot Curry soaks them and pressure-cooks everything in one pot with 1.5 cups water (1 cup for canned).

Chicken Tinga

Smoky shredded chicken for tostadas or tacos.

  • Serves 4
  • 10 min prep
  • 40 min cooking
  • 50 min in all

Ingredients · 4 servings

  • 1.5 lb Boneless skinless chicken thighs
  • 1 White onion
  • 3 cloves Garlic
  • 1 can (15 oz) Tomato sauce
  • 2 chipotles + 2 tbsp adobo Chipotles in adobo buy 1 can (7 oz)
  • 1 tsp Mexican oregano buy 1 jar
  • 1/2 tsp Ground cumin buy 1 jar
  • 2 tbsp Olive oil buy 1 bottle
  • 1 tsp Salt buy 1 box
  • 1/2 tsp Black pepper buy 1 jar
  • 12 Corn tortillas
  • 1 Avocado
  • 1/2 cup Mexican crema buy 1 tub
  • 4 oz Queso fresco
  • 1 Lime

Steps

  1. Pat the 1.5 lb chicken thighs dry and season with 1 tsp salt and 1/2 tsp pepper. Thinly slice the onion and mince the 3 garlic cloves.
  2. Heat 2 tbsp olive oil in a large, deep skillet over medium-high. Lay in the thighs and leave them for 3 minutes, until they’re brown and let go of the pan. Flip them and cook the other side for 2 minutes. They’ll finish cooking in the sauce, so move them to a plate for now.
  3. Turn the heat to medium and add the onion to the same pan. Cook for 5 minutes, scraping up the browned bits, until it’s soft and golden at the edges. Add the garlic, 1 tsp oregano and 1/2 tsp cumin and stir for 30 seconds, until you can smell the garlic.
  4. Blend the can of tomato sauce with 2 chipotles and 2 tbsp of the adobo until smooth. Start with 2 chipotles. You can always add heat at the end, but you can’t take it out. The rest of the can keeps in the fridge.
  5. Pour the sauce over the onions and bring it to a bubble, then put the chicken and its juices back in. Cover, turn the heat to low and simmer for 25 minutes. You’re cooking it until it pulls apart easily with a fork. If you have a thermometer, that’s about 175°F in the thickest piece.
  6. Shred the chicken right in the pan with two forks and stir it through the sauce. Simmer uncovered for 5 minutes so the sauce clings to the chicken. Taste it now, and chop in another chipotle if you’d like more heat.
  7. Warm the 12 tortillas in a dry pan. Pile on the tinga and top with sliced avocado, the 1/2 cup crema, the 4 oz queso fresco crumbled over, and lime wedges.

Mexico in My Kitchen chops fresh tomatoes and chipotles rather than blending and starts from poached shredded chicken. Reviewers on every page say to adjust the chipotles for heat.

Thai Basil Chicken

A fifteen-minute stir-fry with a fried egg on top.

  • Serves 4
  • 10 min prep
  • 15 min cooking
  • 25 min in all

Ingredients · 4 servings

  • 1 lb Ground chicken
  • 3 Thai bird chilies buy 1 package
  • 3 Shallots
  • 5 cloves Garlic
  • 1 bunch Thai basil
  • 4 Eggs
  • 5 tbsp Vegetable oil buy 1 bottle
  • 2 tsp Sugar buy 1 bag
  • 2 tbsp Soy sauce buy 1 bottle
  • 1 tbsp Fish sauce buy 1 bottle
  • 1/3 cup Low-sodium chicken broth
  • 1 1/2 cups Jasmine rice

Steps

  1. Rinse the 1 1/2 cups jasmine rice and get it cooking first. The stir-fry only takes 10 minutes, and it won’t wait for the rice.
  2. Thinly slice the 3 shallots, 5 garlic cloves and 3 Thai chilies. If anyone at the table doesn’t like heat, one chili is plenty. Pick the leaves off the basil. Everything goes in fast, so have it all by the stove.
  3. Fry the eggs first. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a wok or large skillet over medium-high and fry the 4 eggs two at a time for about 2 minutes, spooning the hot oil over the whites, until the edges are brown and lacy and the yolks are still soft. Set them aside.
  4. Turn the heat to high and add the other 3 tbsp oil with the chilies, shallots and garlic. Stir for 1 to 2 minutes, until they’re soft and just starting to brown. Stand back a little. The chili fumes can make you cough.
  5. Add the 1 lb ground chicken and stir-fry for 2 minutes, breaking it into small bits, until there’s no pink left.
  6. Add 2 tsp sugar, 2 tbsp soy sauce and 1 tbsp fish sauce and toss for a minute.
  7. Pour in the 1/3 cup broth (or water). It’ll bubble hard and cook off in under a minute, leaving the chicken glossy.
  8. Take the pan off the heat and stir in all the basil until it wilts, about 20 seconds. Serve over the rice with a fried egg on each plate.

Woks of Life uses 1 lb chicken, 2 tbsp soy, 1 tbsp fish sauce, 2 tsp sugar and deglazes with 1/3 cup broth, 10 minutes start to finish. Hot Thai Kitchen pounds the chilies and garlic into a rough paste and adds oyster sauce and dark soy; both say holy basil is the real thing and Thai basil is a fine stand-in (Hot Thai Kitchen prefers Italian basil as the closer match).

Misir Wat

Red lentils in berbere, cheap, and mostly hands-off simmering.

  • Serves 4
  • 10 min prep
  • 55 min cooking
  • 1 hr 5 min in all

Ingredients · 4 servings

  • 1 cup Red lentils
  • 4 tbsp Ghee buy 1 jar
  • 1 Yellow onion
  • 4 cloves Garlic
  • 1 Roma tomato
  • 2 tbsp Tomato paste buy 1 can (6 oz)
  • 2 tbsp Berbere buy 1 jar
  • 2 1/2 cups Vegetable broth
  • 1 tsp Salt buy 1 box
  • 1 package Injera

Steps

  1. Finely chop the onion, mince the 4 garlic cloves and dice the tomato. Rinse the 1 cup lentils in a sieve until the water runs mostly clear, so the stew doesn’t foam.
  2. Melt 2 tbsp of the ghee (or niter kibbeh, if you have it) in a heavy pot over medium-high. Cook the onion for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring often, until it’s golden. Give it the full time. The onions are the base of the whole stew.
  3. Add the garlic, tomato, 2 tbsp tomato paste and 1 tbsp of the berbere. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until the paste darkens and it smells toasty. If the berbere starts to stick, turn the heat down. Burnt berbere tastes bitter.
  4. Add the lentils, the 2 1/2 cups broth and 1 tsp salt. Bring it to a boil, then cover and simmer on low for 35 to 40 minutes, stirring now and then, until the lentils break down into a thick stew. If it gets too stiff before then, add a splash of water.
  5. Stir in the last 2 tbsp ghee and 1 tbsp berbere and simmer 2 minutes more. That second spoonful of berbere is what gives it a fresh kick of heat. Taste and add salt if it needs it.
  6. Serve with injera to scoop it up, or over rice.

What the sources agree and differ on

All three cook a lot of onion slowly in spiced butter (niter kibbeh) or oil until golden, fry garlic, tomato or tomato paste and berbere in it, then simmer red lentils in broth until they collapse into a thick stew. The Daring Gourmet uses 1 cup lentils to 2 1/2 cups broth, splits both the niter kibbeh and the berbere, and stirs the second half in at the end, simmering about 40 minutes covered. Immaculate Bites adds ginger, coriander or cumin and smoked paprika; Urban Farmie makes it vegan with oil in about 45 minutes, simmering 20-25 minutes with the rest of the berbere added late, and offers paprika, coriander and chili powder if there's no berbere.
