Everyone gets their own sign-in

Each adult signs in with their own email and a 6-digit code, and lands in the same household. Here's every step, one phone per screen. The plain-words version, the build plan and the questions for Emily are in own-logins-plan.md, next to this file.

Step A

Emily signs up and starts the household

The front door asks for an email, never a passphrase. A new email makes a new household; onboarding's first question names the person the email belongs to.

Step B

Emily invites Vineeth

From Settings → People. The link goes out by text, email or whatever the phone offers. It works once, within a week.

Step C

Vineeth opens the link and signs up as himself

The link says which household and which person. His email and code make it his own sign-in. No passphrase, no "Who's this?".

Step D

Vineeth gets a new phone

He signs in with his email and a code — the same front door as A1 and A2 — and he's himself again. Each person can see which phones they're signed in on.

Step E

Moving today's passphrase households over

Emily's, Julia's, Gowthami's and Tester Home. Nothing about the household moves or is copied: each adult's email is attached to the person who's already there, with all their history. The passphrase keeps working until every adult has an email.

Step F

Removing a person

From the same People list. Their phones stop opening the household; everything they planned, added or cooked stays with the household.

What changes for the household

Four things that start telling the truth

Today the app trusts a tap on "Who's this?". Once each person signs in as themselves, these stop being guesses.

Gone

"Who's this?"

Now: after the passphrase, a two-adult house picks a name, and the phone remembers it.

Then: the sign-in is the person. Nobody is asked, and nobody can pick someone else by mistake (or on purpose).

Real

"Approved by Vineeth"

Now: names whoever was picked on the phone that tapped Approve.

Then: names the person who signed in and tapped it. Same for grocery adds, the pre-shop drops and the week's questions.

Addressed

Notifications go to a person

Now: "Emily settled the week" shows on every phone without a pick, and one tap away clears it for the whole house.

Then: it goes to Vineeth, on his phones, and his dismissing it doesn't clear Emily's. The morning text and evening nudge already go to a person's own number.

Possible

"Since you last looked"

Now: not built — it can't be, because the app doesn't know when you last looked.

Then: each sign-in remembers when that person last opened Pomona, so "Emily approved the week, added milk and left you the defrost" can be said to Vineeth, and only what's new to him.
