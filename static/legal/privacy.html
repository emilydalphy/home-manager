Pomona helps a household plan meals, keep the grocery list and know what’s in the kitchen. To do that it has to know some things about the people who live there. This page says what it keeps, who else sees it, and how to get it deleted.

Pomona is run by [Company legal name] (“we”, “us”), [registered address]. Questions about your information go to [privacy contact email].

The short version

  • We keep what your household tells Pomona, so it can plan for you.
  • To plan and chat, Pomona sends your household’s details to Anthropic, the company that makes the Claude AI. It only does that after someone in your household taps Allow, and you can turn it off.
  • No ads. No analytics. No trackers. We don’t sell your information, or share it for advertising.
  • You can delete your whole household from inside the app, any time.

What Pomona keeps

About the people in your household

  • Names, and whether each person is an adult or a child (and an age group, like “toddler”).
  • Allergies, dietary needs, and what each person likes and won’t eat. Some of this is health information, and it can be about children. We treat it with care because of that.
  • Pets’ names and what kind of animal they are.
  • Notes you ask Pomona to remember about your household.
  • A phone number, if you turn on the morning text.
  • The email address each adult signs in with, if they use one.

About how your week runs

  • Who’s home for which meals, on which days, and when people are away. Put plainly, this can show when your home is empty.
  • If you connect a calendar, its private link and the events Pomona reads from it.

About your food

  • Meal plans, recipes, grocery lists, what’s in your kitchen, and shopping trips.
  • Photos of cookbook pages you add a recipe from. Other photos you scan (receipts, your fridge, your pantry, a written list) are read and not kept.

About using the app

  • Your household passphrase, stored only as a one-way hash. Nobody, including us, can read it back.
  • When you sign in with your email, the code we send you is stored only as a one-way hash, and it stops working after 10 minutes. We also note which version of these terms and this policy your household agreed to, and when.
  • Two cookies: one that keeps you signed in, and one that remembers which adult uses this device. Your Light or Dark choice is saved on your device only.
  • If your phone gets notifications from the iPhone app, the address Apple gives us to reach that phone.
  • Counts of how much the AI was used and what it cost, and the names of the actions a chat took. Never the words of your chat.
  • When something breaks: the kind of error and which screen it was on. Never the full web address, and never what you typed.
  • If you use “Something not working?”: what you wrote, which screen you were on, and your browser type.

Your chat with Pomona is saved in our database while you’re in the middle of it, so a conversation isn’t lost if the server restarts. After four hours without a message, Pomona starts the next chat fresh and doesn’t read the old one back. A saved chat is deleted a week after its last message, or straight away if you delete your household. Like the rest of your data, it can stay in the daily backups for up to 15 days after that.

Who else sees it, and why

Anthropic (the AI)

Pomona’s planning and chat are done by Claude, an AI made by Anthropic. To plan your week or answer you, Pomona sends Anthropic what it needs, which is most of your household’s details: names and ages, allergies and dietary needs, what everyone likes, who’s home when, your calendar events, what’s in your kitchen, recent meals, your recipes, what you type in chat, the photos you scan, and the text of recipe pages you bring in from a link.

Pomona asks first. Nothing goes to Anthropic until an adult in your household taps Allow. If you turn it off (Preferences, then Sharing with Claude), nothing more is sent. Planning and chat stop working while it’s off, and nothing you’ve saved is lost.

How long we keep it

As long as your household exists in Pomona. Error records and problem reports are trimmed as they pile up.

We take a copy of the database once a day in case something goes wrong, and keep each copy for 14 days. So after you delete your household, a copy can stay in those backups for up to 15 days, and then it’s gone too.

Deleting your household

In Pomona, open Preferences (the gear at the top of the main screens) and choose Delete your household at the bottom. Everything goes straight away: every person, plan, list, recipe and photo, and the passphrase. There’s no undo. If your household has more than one adult, one of you can leave instead, and your own details go while everyone else’s stay.
