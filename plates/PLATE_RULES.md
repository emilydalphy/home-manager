# Pomona plate rules

These come from Emily's review of the drawings. They apply to every plate, including new ones. When she gives new
feedback on a plate, add it here as a rule and apply it across the library, not only to that plate.

The test for every plate: **cover the name. Could someone tell what this dish is?**

The style: **icons of food, not realistic food.** Plates keep a simple, icon-like feel. They don't need to look real, but it must be clear what each one is. *("It doesn't need to look like real food, but it does need to be clear what it is.")*

## What's on the plate
1. **The main food is plainly visible.** You should see the chicken, not just its sauce. Never let sauce or a side hide it.
   *(Chicken Dijon: "this just looks like dijon and mayo, I can't see the chicken.")*
2. **One hero.** The main food is clearly the biggest thing on the plate, and sides stay smaller.
3. **Build the plate the way the dish is served.** If sauce goes on top of the food, draw it on top.
   *(Chicken Parmesan: sauce on top of a breaded cutlet, not chicken sitting in a red pool.)*
4. **Show the identifying details, and make every one readable.** A side that makes you ask "what is that?" is worse than no side. *("What's this red thing in the corner? Is it strips of bacon?")* Each dish needs the 3 to 5 ingredients that make it that dish, drawn at their real
   shape. Plain flecks are not detail. *(Salade Lyonnaise, Wonton Soup, Banh Mi and others read as weak without them.)*
5. **No more than five foods on a plate.** The well and the sauce don't count. Crowded plates turn to mush at phone size.
6. **Nothing on the plate that isn't in the recipe.** A vegetarian dish never shows anything that looks like meat, and a nut-free dish never shows nuts. People read a plate as a promise, so check this before anything else.
7. **The plate looks full and generous.** No sparse scatter and no small pile on a mostly empty plate.
8. **Food looks delicious.** Rich cooked colours, a seared edge, a glossy shine. If it wouldn't make someone hungry, redraw it. *("Salmon doesn't look appetizing.")*
9. **Food stands out from what's under it.** No dark food on dark sauce and no pale food on a pale base. If the main ingredient melts into the plate, change the sauce or the food until it reads. *(Ma Po Eggplant: "try this again." Beef Chow Fun: "this doesn't look like noodles.")*
10. **Nothing leaves the plate.** All food stays inside the well, including sides and garnishes.

## Kinds of dish
11. **The same food always looks the same.** Broccoli, tomato, rice and every other ingredient keep the same shape and colour on every plate, so the set feels like one family.
12. **Noodles look like noodles.** Long noodles use the `noodles` part: a twirled nest of long strands. Wide noodles use `ribbons`, folded back and forth. Use the noodle the dish really uses: egg noodles are wide ribbons, so stroganoff is never spaghetti. *("Stroganoff is usually a different pasta shape.")* Never a mound or
   short loose strips. Short pasta (penne, macaroni, orecchiette) uses short chunky pieces, and filled pasta shows its folded shape. *("This doesn't look like pasta.")*
   *("This doesn't look like noodles." "This doesn't look enough like noodles.")*
13. **Blended soups are smooth.** One creamy colour with fine flecks of the ingredient, a swirl, and a small garnish in the middle. Never chunks of what was blended. *("The broccoli and cheddar are blended together.")*
14. **Pasta is not soup.** Noodles first, sauce on top. No noodles floating in a full pool of sauce. Noodle soups stay in
   broth with the strands clearly visible. *("This looks more like a soup than a sauce on top of the pasta noodles.")*
15. **Stews look different from soups.** Stews are thick and chunky. Soups are brothy. Curries and stews need the same care as soups so they don't blur together.
16. **Salads look fresh and crisp.** Bright leaves and cut vegetables, never a dull pile.
17. **Desserts read as sweet at a glance.** Baked golds, cream, fruit and chocolate, never savoury colours.
18. **Stir-fries look tossed.** Long strips of the main ingredients, tumbled together in a glossy sauce, with scallion or
   chili on top. Never a clump, a blob or scattered flecks. *("This doesn't look like a stir fry as much.")*
19. **Drinks are drinks.** Anything you drink goes in a glass. *("This should be a drink.")*
20. **Dips never cover what's dipped.** The dip is small or to the side, and the slices, chips or vegetables around it
    are fully visible. *(Apple Slices with Peanut Butter: "this is covering the apple slices.")*
21. **Dishes look like one dish, never rigid.** Food sits together with soft, natural edges, not in straight stripes or neat bands. A rice bowl shows the rice underneath, with toppings piled on it. *("It looks too rigid, and I can't tell it's over rice.")*
22. **Rice is a clear mound, not half a pie chart.** Good-sized and visible, with the food beside it filling the other
    side. Never a straight split down the plate, and never a small cluster off in a corner.
23. **Eggs look like eggs, and only eggs do.** Fried eggs show white and yolk, omelettes look folded. A soup, stew or dal
    must not read as an omelette, so plain yellow discs need toppings that show what they are. *("What's this, an omelette?")*
24. **Show what holds the dish.** Fajitas show the tortilla, a burger shows its bun, a bowl shows its bowl. *("You can't see that this is in a fajita.")*
25. **Use the real shape.** Whole foods keep their natural outline: potatoes are oval, not tubes, a chop looks like a chop, with its bone and fat edge, fish is a flaky fillet with a seared edge, or a whole fish with a tail only when the recipe uses a whole fish, and always stands out from its sauce, shrimp are plump curls with a clear tail, spaced so each one reads, bacon is wavy rashers with pale fat stripes, and eggplant shows purple skin and pale flesh. Shredded food stays shredded: cabbage and lettuce are thin strips, never a solid band, because a band of purple reads as eggplant. *("This looks like eggplant tacos.")* *("Potatoes should be more oval." "Pork chop is weak here." "This doesn't look like fish." "Make this look more shrimp." "Is it strips of bacon?")* Stuffed dishes look stuffed, pastries look like pastries, wraps like wraps, pancakes like
    pancakes, pizza like pizza.
26. **No eyes or faces on food.** Shrimp, fish and everything else stay plain food, never characters. *("Take out the eyes, that's creepy.")*

27. **Porridges are smooth.** Congee, oatmeal and grits are a soft, smooth surface with toppings on it, never noodle-like strips.
28. **A borrowed shape must fit the dish.** A whole-dish part carries only that dish's real fillings. No carrot in a chocolate sandwich.

29. **Meats look like themselves.** Pork is pale pink-brown, beef and lamb are deep brown, chicken is golden, even under a red or brown sauce or glaze, and fried pieces show pale meat inside. *("Doesn't look like chicken.")* Colour alone can't carry it, but the wrong colour reads as the wrong meat. *("This doesn't look like pork.")*

42. **A snack with several parts shows every part.** Apple slices with cheddar and pretzels shows all three, not just the pretzel because it's the easiest to draw. The first food in the name leads, and each named part is drawn at its real shape so you can pick it out at a glance. Yogurt is a white bowl with its toppings on part of it, so the yogurt still shows. *("It's making it look like it's just pretzels." "The image for the greek yogurt and berries is not good.")*

## Across the library
30. **No two dishes look the same.** Colour alone doesn't separate dishes. Curries, stews and thin soups in similar
    colours need different toppings. Whole-dish parts such as sandwiches and tacos need something that identifies this dish.
31. **Not every plate has the same layout.** Rice on one side and food on the other gets repetitive in a list, so layouts vary between dishes.
32. **Match the approved examples.** `examples.json` is the bar for detail and polish. A plate Emily approves goes in it. *("This looks better.")* The 20-recipe sample (Karides Guvec, Ma Po Eggplant and the rest) is the benchmark for the whole library. Approved plates stay as they are, including the few over the five-food limit.
33. **Tweaks keep their plate.** A small change keeps the plate. A change of main ingredient moves to a closer plate.

## How plates are made
34. **Drawn plates, not photos.** Every dish uses a drawing, so the app looks consistent.
35. **Every recipe has a plate before anyone needs it.** The library is pre-filled. New recipes get a plate when they're added.
36. **New dishes reuse first.** A brand-new dish takes the closest existing plate straight away, and a new plate can follow in the background.
37. **Leftovers keep their dish.** Same plate and colours, with a smaller portion.
38. **Plates are built from shared food parts.** Each dish is a short description of parts, so every plate has the same style and can be redrawn.
39. **Plates work on every background.** They sit on cream, on the dusk blue tonight card and later in dark mode, and must read on all of them. On cream and white, every plate gets one soft shadow so its white rim doesn't disappear. On dusk blue and dark mode, the rim stands out by itself, so small plates need no shadow. The shadow comes from the app, not the drawing. *("Add the soft shadows for the lighter colour backgrounds.")*
40. **Readable at phone size.** A plate must still look like its dish in a small row. Fine details can fade, but the dish must not.
41. **Feedback becomes a rule.** Each comment on a plate is fixed, added here, and applied across the library. Comments attach to a whole sheet, so if a comment doesn't show which plate, ask for the dish name.
